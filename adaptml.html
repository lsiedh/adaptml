iAbout this analysis

AdaptML builds a hidden Markov model on the phylogeny, treating habitat at each node as a latent state. An EM loop simultaneously (a) infers each habitat's emission distribution over the observed environmental categories and (b) updates the transition rate μ.

After convergence, a joint (Viterbi-style) assignment maps a single best habitat to every ancestral node. Subtrees rooted at habitat-transition nodes are then tested for significance against an empirical null produced by shuffling the leaf-environment labels.

How to recreate Figure 1 of Hunt et al. (2008)

  1. Click Load vibrio.hsp60.tree. The ecology table fills with the paper's color scheme (size fraction Z/5/1/F, season S/F).
  2. Leave defaults at init_hab_num = 16, collapse_thresh = 0.10, converge_thresh = 0.001. Bump Empirical significance percentile to 0.9999 and Randomization iterations to 10000 for publication-grade significance.
  3. Click Run AdaptML. Watch the progress bar.
  4. Click Download all (.zip). Unzip locally.
  5. At itol.embl.de, upload tree.nwk. Then drag-and-drop each dataset_*.txt onto the iTOL canvas.

Algorithmic defaults

All defaults match the original AdaptML code by Lawrence David (Alm Lab, MIT). The empirical p-value of 0.9999 plus 90 % subtree-coherence cutoff reproduces the alternating blue/gray banding from the paper.
